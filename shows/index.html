Live · 1970 – 1983

The bills.

Led Zeppelin at Kezar. KISS and Rush at Winterland. Frank Zappa at Knebworth. David Bowie in front of sixty thousand people. The shows The Tubes played, and the bands whose names shared the marquee.

Trinity Cathedral · June 1970
Prologue · Before The Tubes 1970

Trinity Cathedral

Phoenix, Arizona · June 1970
  • The BeansBill Spooner’s band

Two years before The Tubes existed, Bill Spooner was running The Beans out of Phoenix with Vince Welnick on keys. This poster is one of the few surviving artifacts from that era — the band would load into a truck for San Francisco, merge with the Red, White & Blues Band, and come out the other side as something else entirely.

Kezar · 6.2.73
The break 1973

Kezar Stadium

San Francisco, California · June 2, 1973
  • Led ZeppelinHeadliner
  • Roy HarperSupport
  • The TubesSupport
  • Lee MichaelsSupport

The show that changed everything. A year before their first record deal, The Tubes walked onto a stadium stage in their own backyard ahead of Led Zeppelin at the peak of the band’s powers. Fee Waybill went out as an early version of Quay Lewd, hurling flour and candy into the crowd as “cocaine” and “pills.” Management came calling shortly after.

Winterland · 10.31.74
Halloween 1974

Winterland Ballroom

San Francisco, California · October 31, 1974
  • The TubesHeadliner
  • CrossfireSupport
  • LightyearSupport

Bill Graham had been wary of the band after Kezar. He got over it. Halloween at Winterland became a Tubes institution — the one night of the year when a stage show built on costume, spectacle and bad taste needed no explaining to anybody.

The marquee · Steiner St, SF
On the marquee 1975

Winterland Ballroom

San Francisco, California · June 1, 1975
  • KissHeadliner

Kiss on the Dressed to Kill tour, The Tubes and a young Rush underneath them, and a line down Steiner Street. The photograph is the Winterland marquee itself on the day — TONIGHT / KISS / TUBES  RUSH — which makes it one of the few surviving bills for a Tubes show that exists as a photograph rather than a print.

London · November 1977

Six sold-out nights at the Hammersmith Odeon — and a council that tried to stop them.

Hammersmith · 11.77
The invasion 1977

Hammersmith Odeon

London, England · November 1977
  • The TubesHeadliner · 6 nights

The Tubes booked a week at one of London’s most storied theatres and sold out all six nights. The run was recorded and released as What Do You Want From Live in February 1978 — the document of the stage show at its absolute peak: mock bondage, exploding televisions, chainsaws, Quay Lewd on his platform boots, and a city that had never seen anything like it.

Portsmouth · 11.77
Banned 1977

Portsmouth Guildhall

Portsmouth, England · November 1977
  • The TubesCancelled by the council

Scheduled for Remembrance Sunday, and that was the end of it. Portsmouth council sent officials all the way to Newcastle to watch the tour’s opening night and decide for themselves whether the show could go ahead. It could not. The ban made national news, which did the ticket sales everywhere else no harm at all.

Knebworth · 9.9.78
The festival 1978

Knebworth Park

Hertfordshire, England · September 9, 1978
  • Frank ZappaJoint headliner
  • The TubesJoint headliner · closed the show
  • Peter GabrielSupport
  • The Boomtown RatsSupport
  • RockpileNick Lowe & Dave Edmunds
  • Wilko Johnson’s Solid SendersSupport

Billed, gloriously, as “Oh God, Not Another Boring Old Knebworth.” Zappa and The Tubes were announced as joint headliners — and on the day, it was The Tubes who closed the festival. Peter Gabriel spent his set climbing the stage scaffolding with a large stuffed panda. One of the great strange bills of the decade, and arguably the high-water mark of the band’s standing in Britain.

Remote Control · ’79
Remote Control tour 1979

Hammersmith Odeon

London, England · 1979
  • The TubesHeadliner
  • SqueezeSupport

Back at Hammersmith behind Remote Control, the Todd Rundgren–produced concept record about television eating the culture. Squeeze opened, a year out from Argybargy. Album sales came in under expectations and the budget got cut, so the legendary production had to be pared back — the beginning of a leaner era.

1983 · The stadium year

Sixty thousand
people deep.

“She’s a Beauty” hit number 10 on the Billboard Hot 100 in 1983, and the rooms changed overnight. Two shows that summer put The Tubes in front of the largest audiences of their career.

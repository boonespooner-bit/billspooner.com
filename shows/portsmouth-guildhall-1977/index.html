Banned 1977

Portsmouth Guildhall

Portsmouth, England · Remembrance Sunday, 13 November 1977
  • The TubesCancelled by the council

Five members of Portsmouth’s Leisure Services Committee — average age 47 — travelled to Birmingham to watch the band and decide for themselves. They came back and banned the show in half an hour.

The vote went four to one. “The sexual simulation was too explicit, the violence unnecessary and the language bad,” said Charles Mos, who allowed that the audience had been well behaved and the performance “electrifying,” but wouldn’t want his two teenage daughters to see it. Mrs Elsie Fudge, 63, a magistrate, thought the music good and the sex scenes “totally unnecessary.” Donald Gray, 66, was convinced the audience “would have applauded just as much if the dirty parts had been left out.”

The one dissenter was the youngest, 30-year-old Terry Gregory: “The music was fantastic — out of this world. There was nothing in the act that was as obscene as we were led to believe.” He was backed by the city’s own entertainments manager, Roy Radford, the man who had booked the group: “Everyone in the audience enjoyed it without any doubt at all.” They were outvoted. The ban made national news, which did ticket sales everywhere else no harm at all.

The Guildhall got them in the end. Its own history of the decade lists the band among the venue’s visitors in July 1979 — filed, a little grudgingly, as “the American punk (ish) band the Tubes.”
